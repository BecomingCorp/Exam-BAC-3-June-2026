# EU Functioning — Institutions and Organisation
> How the EU institutions are structured and how they interact in practice

---

## PART 1 — COMPLETE SYNTHESIS

## KEY CONCEPT 1 — The 3 Circles: EU, Schengen, Eurozone

The EU is not a monolithic bloc — it has 3 overlapping membership circles:

| Circle | Number of states | What it means |
|--------|-----------------|---------------|
| **EU** | 27 states | Full membership, Single Market, EU law applies |
| **Schengen** | 29 states | Free movement of people, no systematic border controls |
| **Eurozone** | 20 states | Single currency (euro), ECB monetary policy |

**Key confusions to avoid:**
- Ireland is in the EU but NOT in Schengen
- Norway, Switzerland, Iceland, Liechtenstein are in Schengen but NOT in the EU
- Bulgaria, Romania, Hungary are in the EU but NOT in the Eurozone (yet)

---

## KEY CONCEPT 2 — The Institutional Triangle (THE exam question)

The EU is governed by 3 institutions that share legislative power in a carefully balanced triangle:

| Institution | Role | Who leads | Location |
|-------------|------|-----------|----------|
| **European Commission** | Executive: proposes laws, enforces treaties, manages budget | Ursula von der Leyen (27 Commissioners, one per state) | Brussels (Berlaymont) |
| **European Parliament** | Co-legislator representing **citizens** | Roberta Metsola (705 MEPs, elected every 5 years) | Strasbourg + Brussels |
| **Council of the EU** | Co-legislator representing **governments** | Rotating 6-month presidency (ministers of relevant portfolios) | Brussels (Justus Lipsius) |

**How to remember the logic:**
- Commission = **proposes** (civil servant logic — technical, not political)
- Parliament = **represents people** (democratic legitimacy)
- Council = **represents states** (intergovernmental logic)

**Commission independence:** Commissioners must NOT take instructions from their home government. They represent the EU interest, not national interests.

---

## KEY CONCEPT 3 — Other Key Institutions (Not in the Triangle)

| Institution | Role | Key detail |
|-------------|------|-----------|
| **European Council** | Sets political direction (heads of state/government) | NOT a legislator — sets the agenda, does NOT pass laws. President: Charles Michel |
| **ECB** | Manages euro monetary policy | Independent, based in Frankfurt. Christine Lagarde |
| **CJEU** | Ensures EU law is respected and interpreted uniformly | Luxembourg — can fine Member States |
| **Court of Auditors** | Audits EU budget | Luxembourg — financial watchdog |
| **Council of Europe** | Human rights body, 46 states | **NOT an EU institution** — completely separate. ECHR jurisdiction |

**Critical trap:** European Council ≠ Council of the EU ≠ Council of Europe. Three completely different bodies.

---

## KEY CONCEPT 4 — The Ordinary Legislative Procedure (OLP)

How most EU laws are made:

```
Commission proposes → Parliament 1st reading → Council 1st reading
                                    ↓
              If agreement → Law adopted ✓
                                    ↓
              If no agreement → Parliament 2nd reading
                                    ↓
              If no agreement → Council 2nd reading
                                    ↓
              If still no agreement → Conciliation Committee
                                    ↓
              If committee fails → Law dies ✗
```

**Qualified Majority in Council:** 55% of states + 65% of EU population (double threshold — protects small AND large countries).

**Unanimity** still required for: taxation, foreign policy, constitutional changes.

---

## KEY CONCEPT 5 — The Rotating Presidency

Every 6 months, a different EU member state holds the **Presidency of the Council of the EU**.

**What the Presidency does:**
- Chairs all Council meetings
- Sets the agenda and priorities for those 6 months
- Mediates between governments to build consensus
- Example: Cyprus held the Presidency in 2012 with priorities: accession negotiations, EU budget, migration

**What the Presidency does NOT do:**
- Cannot propose legislation (that's the Commission)
- Cannot vote differently from its national position
- Cannot make deals without Council agreement

**Why it matters:** Small countries can have outsized influence during their presidency — they set the work program and can accelerate their priorities.

---

## KEY CONCEPT 6 — EU Decision-Making: Types of Majority

| Type | When used | Rule |
|------|-----------|------|
| **Simple majority** | Procedural matters | >50% of members |
| **Qualified majority (QMV)** | Most legislation | 55% of states + 65% of population |
| **Unanimity** | Sensitive areas: tax, foreign policy, EU enlargement | All 27 must agree |
| **Enhanced cooperation** | If at least 9 states want to go further | Allowed in most policy areas |

**Blocking minority:** To block a QMV decision, you need 4+ states representing 35%+ of EU population.

---

## KEY CONCEPT 7 — Types of EU Competences

The EU doesn't have power over everything. Powers are divided:

| Type | What it means | Examples |
|------|--------------|---------|
| **Exclusive** | Only the EU acts | Trade policy, customs union, competition rules, monetary policy (eurozone) |
| **Shared** | Both EU and states, states act where EU doesn't | Internal market, agriculture, transport, environment |
| **Supporting** | EU supports but doesn't replace national action | Education, culture, tourism, sport |

**The 3 governing principles:**
1. **Conferral**: EU only has powers explicitly given by treaties
2. **Subsidiarity**: EU acts only if states cannot act effectively alone
3. **Proportionality**: EU action must not exceed what's needed

---

## KEY CONCEPT 8 — The European Parliament in Detail

**705 MEPs** (will change to 720 post-2024 election) from 27 states, elected every 5 years by direct universal suffrage.

**Parliament's powers:**
- Co-legislative power (equal with Council under OLP)
- Budget approval and control (can reject the budget)
- Elects and can dismiss the European Commission
- Democratic scrutiny of EU institutions

**MEPs sit in political groups** (not national delegations):
- EPP (center-right — largest group)
- S&D (socialists)
- Renew Europe (liberals)
- ECR, ID (right/far-right)
- Greens/EFA
- The Left

**Strasbourg vs Brussels:** Plenary sessions in Strasbourg (12x/year — historical seat), committee work in Brussels.

---

## KEY CONCEPT 9 — The Commission: Guardian of the Treaties

**28 Commissioners** (27 + President), one per state, allocated portfolios.

**Key roles:**
1. **Sole right of legislative initiative** — only the Commission can formally propose legislation
2. **Guardian of treaties** — can take states to the CJEU for violating EU law
3. **Executive of the EU** — manages budget, implements EU law
4. **External representative** — negotiates trade deals on behalf of all EU states

**Infringement procedure:** Commission notices violation → formal notice → reasoned opinion → CJEU referral → possible fine.

**Example:** In 2022, the Commission launched infringement proceedings against Hungary over rule of law violations.

---

## KEY CONCEPT 10 — EU Budget

**Multiannual Financial Framework (MFF):** 7-year budget plan. Current MFF 2021-2027: ~€1,074 billion.

**Main spending categories:**
- Cohesion (regional development): ~33%
- Agriculture (CAP): ~31%
- Research & Innovation: ~10%
- Neighbourhood & the World: ~9%

**Revenue sources:**
- GNI-based contribution by member states (~70%)
- Customs duties and import levies (~14%)
- VAT-based own resource (~10%)
- New: carbon border adjustment, digital levy (proposed)

## PART 2 — Q&A EXAM FORMAT

**Q1: What are the 3 circles of European membership? How many states in each?**
> A: EU = 27 states (full membership). Schengen = 29 states (free movement, includes non-EU Norway, Switzerland, Iceland, Liechtenstein). Eurozone = 20 states (single currency). The 3 circles overlap but are not identical — Ireland is EU but not Schengen; Norway is Schengen but not EU.

**Q2: What are the 3 institutions of the Institutional Triangle and what are their respective roles?**
> A: European Commission (executive: proposes laws, enforces treaties — von der Leyen, 27 Commissioners). European Parliament (co-legislator representing citizens — Metsola, 705 MEPs directly elected). Council of the EU (co-legislator representing member state governments — rotating 6-month presidency, relevant ministers).

**Q3: European Council vs Council of the EU vs Council of Europe — explain the difference.**
> A: European Council = heads of state/government (Macron, Scholz…), sets political direction, NOT a legislator. Council of the EU = ministers of 27 governments, co-legislates with Parliament. Council of Europe = completely separate from EU, 46 countries, defends human rights (ECHR) — NOT an EU institution.

**Q4: How does the Ordinary Legislative Procedure (OLP) work?**
> A: Commission proposes → Parliament and Council co-decide on equal footing, up to 3 readings each. If no agreement after all readings, a Conciliation Committee is convened. If the committee still fails, the law dies. This process ensures democratic legitimacy (Parliament) and state consent (Council).

**Q5: What is qualified majority voting (QMV) in the Council?**
> A: A decision passes with QMV when: 55% of member states agree AND those states represent at least 65% of total EU population. This double threshold protects both small states (55% rule) and large states (65% population rule). To block a QMV decision: 4+ states representing 35%+ of population.

**Q6: What is the role of the European Commission as "guardian of the treaties"?**
> A: The Commission monitors whether member states comply with EU law. If a state violates EU law, the Commission can launch infringement proceedings: formal notice → reasoned opinion → CJEU referral → financial penalties. Example: fining states for not transposing directives, or for restricting free movement.

**Q7: Why is the Commission said to be independent?**
> A: Commissioners must act in the EU's general interest, not under instructions from their home government. They swear an oath of independence. This is why the Commission can fine even large member states or block national policies that violate EU law.

**Q8: What is the rotating presidency of the Council? What can it do and not do?**
> A: Every 6 months, a different member state chairs all Council of the EU meetings, sets the agenda, and mediates between governments. It CANNOT propose legislation (only the Commission can). Example: Cyprus Presidency 2012 prioritized accession negotiations and the EU budget framework.

**Q9: What are the 3 principles governing EU competences? Define each.**
> A: Conferral: the EU only has powers explicitly granted by the founding treaties. Subsidiarity: the EU acts only when objectives cannot be achieved effectively at national or regional level. Proportionality: EU action must not exceed what is necessary to achieve the treaty objective.

**Q10: What is the difference between exclusive, shared, and supporting competences?**
> A: Exclusive: only the EU acts (trade policy, customs, competition, eurozone monetary policy). Shared: both EU and states can act, but states act where EU hasn't (agriculture, internal market, environment, transport). Supporting: EU complements national action without replacing it (education, culture, sport, tourism).

**Q11: What powers does the European Parliament have beyond legislation?**
> A: Budget power (Parliament must approve the 7-year MFF and annual budgets; can reject them). Political control: Parliament elects the Commission President and can vote a motion of censure to dismiss the entire Commission. Democratic scrutiny: MEPs question Commissioners, hold hearings.

**Q12: What is an infringement procedure?**
> A: A formal process launched by the Commission when a member state violates EU law. Steps: (1) Letter of formal notice — state must respond. (2) Reasoned opinion — Commission explains the breach. (3) CJEU referral — court can impose fines (lump sum + daily penalty). Example: Poland fined €1M/day over judicial independence violations.

**Q13: How is the EU budget financed?**
> A: Three main "own resources": 1. GNI-based contributions from states (~70% of budget). 2. Traditional own resources: customs duties on imports from non-EU countries (~14%). 3. VAT-based resource (~10%). New sources being added: carbon border adjustment mechanism, digital levy.

**Q14: What is the Multiannual Financial Framework (MFF)?**
> A: A 7-year budget plan that sets spending ceilings for each EU policy area. Current MFF: 2021-2027 (€1,074 billion). It must be approved unanimously by the Council and by the Parliament. It provides budgetary stability and predictability for EU programs.

**Q15: How does the EU Parliament ensure democratic accountability?**
> A: MEPs are directly elected by EU citizens every 5 years (proportional representation). Parliament can: reject the Commission as a whole (censure motion, requiring 2/3 majority + absolute majority). Question Commissioners at hearings. Reject or amend the EU budget. Co-decide legislation affecting all 450 million EU citizens — making it the only directly elected supranational parliament in the world.
